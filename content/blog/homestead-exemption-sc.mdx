Qualifying prospective and current South Carolina residents could benefit from a property tax exemption. Basically, an exemption means free from property taxes. Fortunately, there is a great property tax savings for SC homeowners who are over 65 years old, totally and permanently disabled, or legally blind. Meeting these criteria could mean using the lifetime Homestead Exemption SC program. That’s right, lifetime! Which means, once qualified for the exemption, homeowners do not have to continually apply each year.

## How Much is the Homestead Exemption SC?

The SC Homestead Exemption is not totally free property taxes for most properties. But, it is an exemption for the first $50,000 in property tax fair market value. Besides the requirements mentioned above, the property must be a primary residence. Additionally, it must be considered the homeowner’s legal residence by the SC county which the property is located.

## Home Exemption SC Requirements

In order to qualify for free property taxes up to the first $50,000 in tax market value, there are specific requirements. The following must apply based on December 31 for the preceding year…

-   Must be the deed owner, life estate, or beneficiary to trust holding title, and
-   Legal resident of SC for one calendar year, and
-   65 years old, or
-   Totally and permanently disabled by a state or federal agency with the ability to determine such, or
-   Certified legally blind by a licensed ophthalmologist

So, SC buyers cannot take advantage of the property tax exemption right after closing. Because of the the preceding year requirements mentioned, it could take up to a year prior to exemption application.

## How to Apply for SC Homestead Exemption

Assuming a homeowner believes he/she qualifies for the tax exemption, there is a process to the application. First of all, every South Carolina county participates in this tax exemption. In order to apply for the Homestead Exemption, homeowners should gather the required documentation and provide it to their local county auditor’s office.

[Learn SC Tax Rates Here](/blog/sc-tax/)

### Homestead Exemption Required Documentation

-   SC driver’s license or birth certificate (if applying for being 65 or older)
-   Documentation proving disability from federal or state agency (if applying because of disability)
-   Certification of blindness from licensed ophthalmologist (if applying because of blindness)
-   Copy of trust agreement (if the home the home is in a trust)

There are a couple other potential advantages when it comes to the Homestead Exemption. For instance, a SC resident who is selling a current qualified residence and buying another one could continue the exemption. Additionally, a surviving spouse who is on title within 9 months after the qualifying spouse’s death, continues to occupy the home, and remains unmarried, may receive the tax exemption.
